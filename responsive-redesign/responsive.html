Overview

Government websites are known to be outdated and utilitarian, so I explored the websites of the cities in Rhode Island. I found the website for the town of Tiverton, a small beach town 25 miles from my school. Despite the dated look and some odd design choices, I liked the overall color palette and functionality of the site, so I sought out to redesign the site to address some of the issues I found, creating a site that was also user-friendly on various common screen sizes. You can access my final redesign here.

Analyzing the Original

The original site is located at tiverton.ri.gov. I've included a snapshot of the landing page that I will redesign below:

Problems

I began by flipping through the site and interacting with it to check for general issues. Here are some of the problems I found, they were primarily visual issues:

  • Text in the top left has very low contrast with the background, can’t even tell it’s there without looking closely.
  • Menu text is small, users have learned that menus on the left side are usually collapsible, not the case for this one.
  • Awkward large blank space at top of screen between town name text and American flag.
  • Menu options do different actions when clicked on, some expand, some redirect to a new page, and others change what’s displayed on the right.
  • Users learn from other webpages that they can usually swipe through slideshows, this one can only change slides by clicking the slide number or next/previous buttons.

Sometimes it's not enough to simply look at a site to detect all the issues. A lot of accessibility problems can be detected by using tools to scan through the HTML content of the webpage. I used webAIM WAVE to detect additional accessibility problems. I found that some of the contrast issues it raised weren't completely accurate:

  • WAVE detected many images missing alternative text, making the site less accessible for screen reader users.
  • The text box to search the site doesn’t have a form label, which also affects screen reader accessibility.
  • WAVE flagged the left menu text as having very low contrast, but visually, the white text on a dark blue background is pretty readable.

With components and a color palette to adhere to, it was time to create mockups in Figma. Implementing the Redesign

I developed the new site completely in HTML and CSS, using colors from my style guide and double checking to make sure that all aspects of the site were easily readable. For the dynamic aspect, I utilized media queries, changing the amount of columns present in the grid style CSS interface I chose to use. You can interact with my redesigned site here. Next, I will showcase what the site looks like for users of different screen sizes.

Desktop Users

  • Updated search bar that users can select to search either town site or RI.gov site
  • Common nav bar that users commonly see, yellow arrows indicate dropdowns that appear on hover
  • Three columns to display more content at the top

Tablet Users

  • Hamburger icon instead of nav bar to avoid screen clutter on smaller screen. On the site, the hamburger icon is just a dummy button.
  • Assistance box gets moved into hamburger menu to make space for the carousel to remain the main focus at the top of the body.

Mobile Users

  • Body collapses into single column, common scrollable interface that mobile users are accustomed to
  • Search bar and contact us become buttons at top of screen that users can easily click when desired
  • Clicking any of the buttons at the top would expand a separate screen over the site's content, but now they are simply dummy buttons

Final Thoughts

Here are my key takeaways from redesigning Tiverton's website:

  • Designing a dynamic webpage that looks good for various screen sizes is surprisingly frustrating (especially in pure html/css)
  • You can't always trust accessibility tools, they sometimes exaggerate issues, or they will suggest high contrast text colors that aren't aesthetically pleasing
  • It's hard to strike a balance between a website that feels fresh/new and one that users can easily navigate
